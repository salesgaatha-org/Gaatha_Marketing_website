How to sell on Blinkit: a brand’s step-by-step guide

By gaa-tha· 25 September 2026· 5 min read· Commerce
Blog cover · 16:9

To sell on Blinkit, a brand applies through the platform’s seller or brand-partner route, passes a category review, submits business and brand documents, uploads a spec-compliant catalogue, agrees commercial terms and fulfils a first purchase order into the cities chosen for launch. The process takes several weeks. This outline covers each step and the first-month priorities that decide whether the listing survives.

Key takeaways

  • Blinkit lists brands that fit its categories and show demand; existing retail or online sales help.
  • Documents, barcodes and images are the usual causes of delay; prepare them before applying.
  • Launch narrow: a few hero SKUs in the cities where you already sell.
  • Fill rate in the first month matters more than ad spend.

Who can sell on Blinkit?

Blinkit works with brands and their authorised distributors across grocery, snacks, beverages, personal care, home care, baby care, pet supplies and a growing range of general merchandise. The platform’s category teams look for products with proven velocity, compact packaging and margins that can carry platform terms and promotions.

New brands are not excluded, but they need a clear story: where the product sells today, why a Blinkit customer would pick it, and which packs are built for a ten-minute basket.

What documents and data does Blinkit onboarding need?

Expect to provide GST registration, PAN, bank details, a trademark certificate or brand authorisation letter, an FSSAI licence for food and beverages, and product-level data such as EAN barcodes, MRP, shelf life, dimensions and weight. Requirements vary by category and can change; confirm the current checklist with the platform or with your agency before submitting.

Mismatched barcodes and missing licence numbers are the most common reasons an application stalls. A single master sheet per SKU prevents most of it.

How should the catalogue and images be prepared?

Blinkit listings are seen as small thumbnails in a fast scroll, so the pack shot must be clean, front-facing and legible at a small size. Titles should carry the brand, product and pack size; descriptions cover ingredients, usage and claims that the label supports. Our q-commerce catalogue and creative service produces these to spec, and product photography handles the shoot.

How do city and dark-store mapping and the first purchase order work?

After commercial terms are agreed, the brand and the platform decide which cities go live first. Stock is then supplied against a purchase order to the platform’s warehouse or distribution point, from where it moves to dark stores. Start with the cities where you already have demand so early velocity data is strong; expansion follows evidence, not ambition.

What should a brand do in the first month after go-live?

Watch availability per store weekly, respond to replenishment POs fast, collect early ratings, and run a small search campaign on your own brand terms. Review per-SKU velocity at the end of the month and prune or add packs accordingly. A regional snack brand with a large range, such as Charliee, would typically launch a short list of hero packs rather than the full catalogue.

Ongoing work such as PO coordination, promotions, ads and reconciliation is what Blinkit account management covers; onboarding itself is described on our Blinkit seller onboarding page.
