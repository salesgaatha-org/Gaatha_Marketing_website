01 Marketing basics

Marketing is the work of understanding what people want, building something worth wanting, and making sure the right people know about it at the moment they care. Advertising is only the visible tip of it.

A complete marketing function covers five things: research (who is the customer and what do they actually need), positioning (why you and not the alternative), product and pricing, distribution (where they can buy), and promotion (how they hear about you). Businesses that treat marketing as only the last item tend to pay more for worse results. Read the full explainer →

A marketing strategy is the set of choices about who you serve, what you stand for, and how you will reach them — written down before any campaign starts. It answers four questions: which segment, what promise, which channels, and what success looks like in numbers.

A strategy is not a list of posts or a content calendar. Those are tactics. Strategy is what makes the tactics agree with each other. See the full framework →

Start by narrowing rather than broadening: pick one clear audience, one sharp promise, and two channels you can do properly instead of six you do badly. Then build a feedback loop — measure one primary metric per campaign, and change one variable at a time.

The biggest practical gains for most Indian SMEs come from four moves: fix your positioning, make your offer concrete, publish content that answers real customer questions, and follow up leads faster. Read the 10-step guide →

Traditional marketing buys attention in physical or broadcast spaces — print, hoardings, radio, TV — and measures reach by estimate. Digital marketing reaches people through search, social, email and web, and measures behaviour directly: who clicked, who bought, at what cost.

The practical difference is feedback speed. Digital lets you test a message for a few thousand rupees and know within days whether it works. Most brands today use both, with digital carrying measurement and traditional carrying credibility.

A common working range is 5-10% of revenue for established businesses and 15-20% for brands in a growth or launch phase — but the more useful question is what a customer is worth to you. If a customer is worth ₹10,000 in lifetime profit, a ₹1,500 acquisition cost is excellent and a ₹12,000 one is fatal.

Start by calculating customer lifetime value and target cost per acquisition. Budget follows those two numbers, not a percentage rule of thumb.

Pick one primary metric per objective. For awareness, use reach and branded search volume. For consideration, use engagement rate, time on page and returning visitors. For revenue, use qualified leads, cost per acquisition and return on ad spend.

Vanity metrics — follower counts, impressions, likes in isolation — tell you a post did something, not that the business grew. Track them as diagnostics, never as goals.

02 Branding & design

Branding is the work of deciding what your business means to people — the name, the look, the voice, the promise. Marketing is the work of getting that meaning in front of the right audience and turning attention into revenue.

Branding builds memory; marketing builds momentum. Get the order wrong and you spend money making people notice something they immediately forget. Read the comparison →

Graphic design is the craft of arranging type, image, colour and space so that information is understood and felt in the way it was intended. It is visual problem-solving, not decoration — a good design makes a message clearer, faster and more trustworthy.

In practice it spans identity design (logos, brand systems), packaging, editorial and layout, advertising creative, motion graphics and digital interface design. Read the full guide →

Graphic design today is systems-first and motion-native. Designers are increasingly asked to deliver a flexible kit — type scale, colour tokens, component rules, motion behaviour — rather than a single static artwork, because the same identity has to work on a phone screen, a package, a reel and an AI-generated ad variant.

The second shift is AI as a production tool. Generative tools now handle variation, resizing and rough concepting, which moves the designer's value up the stack: art direction, taste, brand judgement and knowing what not to make. Read more →

A good logo is distinctive, legible at every size, and honest about what the business is. The practical test: it must survive a 16-pixel favicon and a forty-foot hoarding, work in one colour, and still look unmistakably like you.

A logo is a signature, not the whole brand. Colour, type, tone of voice and consistency do most of the recognition work over time.

For small businesses and startups in India, a basic logo package typically runs from a few thousand rupees to around ₹50,000, while a full identity system — naming support, logo, colour and type system, applications and guidelines — generally starts around ₹50,000 and rises with scope.

The variable is depth, not drawing time: strategy, research, application design and documentation are where most of the cost and most of the long-term value sit. gaa-tha scopes identity work to the business stage rather than a fixed price list.

Add the app ecosystem for payments, shipping, reviews and WhatsApp, and a small team can run a serious store without a developer on call. gaa-tha sets these up as part of every Shopify build so they are actually used, not just available.

The most under-used Shopify features are metaobjects for reusable content such as ingredient or size-guide libraries, Shopify Flow for free automations like order tagging and low-stock alerts, bulk URL redirect import, customer segments for targeted Shopify Email campaigns and discounts, draft orders for phone and WhatsApp sales, and the Search & Discovery app for synonyms, filters and product boosts.

Many stores pay for apps that duplicate something Shopify already does natively, so an audit of these features often cuts app costs as well as adding capability.

Yes — Shopify is one of the most practical ecommerce platforms for Indian D2C brands because it is hosted, secure and fast to launch, and it works with Indian payment gateways, COD, UPI, shipping aggregators like Shiprocket and GST invoicing apps.

The main India-specific caveat is that Shopify Payments and Shop Pay are not generally available to Indian-registered stores, so checkout runs through gateways such as Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree or CCAvenue, or one-click checkouts such as GoKwik, Shiprocket Checkout and Razorpay Magic Checkout. Why Shopify for ecommerce →

Choose Shopify if you want to sell quickly with low maintenance, WooCommerce if you already run WordPress and want full control of hosting and plugins, and a custom build only when your model — a multi-vendor marketplace, complex B2B pricing or unusual workflows — genuinely does not fit a platform.

Shopify charges a monthly fee but removes hosting, security and update work; WooCommerce is cheaper to start but needs ongoing maintenance; custom code costs the most to build and to own. For most Indian D2C brands, Shopify is the default and the others are exceptions.
