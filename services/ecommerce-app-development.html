The service

What is Ecommerce & Sales App Development?

Ecommerce and sales app development covers mobile apps that take orders: shopping apps that let consumers browse and buy from a brand directly, B2B ordering apps that let retailers and distributors reorder from their own price lists, and sales-force automation (SFA) apps that field reps use to visit outlets, book orders and log collections. All three share a core — catalogue, prices, cart and order — but each serves a different user with different business rules.

At gaa-tha these apps are built around the system that already runs the business. A D2C app reads products, stock and customers from Shopify so the store and the app never disagree; a B2B or field-sales app syncs with Tally or your ERP so prices, schemes and outstanding balances are right before a rep walks into a shop. Because we also run D2C marketing, push, WhatsApp and loyalty journeys are planned with the app, not bolted on later.

Scope

What's included

  • D2C shopping app on Shopify via the Storefront API — collections, search, filters, wishlist, cart and customer accounts in sync with your store
  • Custom catalogue backend where there is no Shopify store, with product, price and inventory management
  • Checkout with UPI, cards, wallets and COD through Razorpay or your existing checkout provider, with coupons and offers
  • B2B ordering app: customer-specific price lists, minimum order quantities, credit limits, quick reorder, order history and invoice downloads
  • Field-sales app: beat plans, GPS check-in, outlet onboarding, order booking, schemes and discounts, collections and shelf photos
  • Offline mode for weak-network areas, with conflict-safe sync when the phone reconnects
  • Tally Prime, ERP and distributor management system (DMS) sync for items, rates, stock, orders and outstanding balances
  • Loyalty points, referrals and tiered rewards, with push notifications segmented by buying behaviour
  • WhatsApp Business API messages for order confirmations, dispatch updates and reorder reminders
  • Manager dashboards for sales by rep, beat, outlet and SKU, plus App Store and Play Store publishing

Process

How we work

Five steps, in the order they happen.

01

Map the order flow

We follow an order from the buyer or rep through to dispatch and invoice, list the rules for prices, schemes, credit and GST, and decide which system is the source of truth.

02

Design for the real user

Screens are designed for the person holding the phone: a shopper on a mid-range Android, a retailer reordering between customers, or a rep with one hand free and patchy signal.

03

Build and integrate

The app, backend and admin are built in sprints, and the Shopify, Tally or ERP, payment and WhatsApp integrations are tested with real data, not samples.

04

Pilot

A shopping app goes first to a small group of loyal customers and a sales app to one territory or a few reps; we fix what they find before the wider rollout.

05

Launch and grow

Store publishing, team training and rollout follow, then push, loyalty and reorder journeys are tuned month by month from usage data.

Deliverables you receive

  • Designed and tested screens for each type of user
  • iOS and Android apps published under your developer accounts
  • Admin panel and manager dashboard
  • Working integrations with Shopify, Tally or ERP, payments and WhatsApp

Good to know

Questions about Ecommerce & sales apps

Answer-first, so humans and AI assistants get the point quickly.

Most D2C brands should wait on an app until repeat customers are a meaningful share of revenue, because an app mainly serves people who already buy from you. It earns its cost when products are reordered often — food, beauty, pet care, household essentials — and when push notifications and loyalty can replace part of your remarketing spend. Until then, a fast mobile website and WhatsApp journeys usually return more.

A Shopify-connected app reads products, collections, prices, inventory and customer accounts from Shopify's Storefront API, so anything you change in the Shopify admin appears in the app without double entry. Orders placed in the app land in Shopify like any other order, so fulfilment, Shiprocket and your reports keep working as they do today.

A subscription app builder is a sensible start when you want a standard shopping app quickly and your store has no unusual logic. A custom app makes sense when you need your own features — kit builders, subscriptions, B2B pricing, loyalty linked to offline stores — or want to stop paying a fee that grows with your revenue. We compare both against your roadmap before recommending either.

A field-sales or sales-force automation app is what sales reps use on their phones to follow their beat, check in at outlets, show the catalogue, book orders with the right scheme, record payments and capture shelf photos. Managers see orders, visits and productivity by rep and territory as they happen, instead of collecting orders on paper or WhatsApp at the end of the day.

Yes. The catalogue, prices, schemes and each rep's outlet list are stored on the phone, orders are saved offline, and everything syncs when the network returns, with checks so the same order is not booked twice. This matters in tier-2 and tier-3 towns, rural beats and inside large stores where signal drops.

Yes. We connect apps to Tally Prime, Zoho, Odoo, ERPNext, SAP Business One or a custom ERP so items, rates, stock, customer balances and orders flow both ways. The method — an API, a connector or a scheduled sync — depends on your system and version, which we check during discovery.

A Shopify-connected shopping app usually takes six to ten weeks, and a B2B ordering or field-sales app eight to sixteen. Cost is driven by the number of user roles, the depth of Tally or ERP integration, offline sync, pricing and scheme rules, and how much loyalty and reporting the first release needs. We scope a pilot version first so you see the app working before committing to the full build.

You own all of it. The apps are published under your developer accounts, the backend runs in cloud accounts in your name, and the source code is handed over in your repository. Customer and sales data stays in your systems rather than a shared platform.
