## What is SaaS Product Development?

SaaS product development is the work of building software that many customers use on subscription: one codebase serving many separate accounts, called tenants, each with its own users, roles, data and billing plan, running reliably in the cloud and improving through frequent releases. Beyond features, a SaaS product needs sign-up and onboarding, billing and invoicing, an internal admin, usage analytics, security and room to scale without a rewrite.

At gaa-tha we treat the first months as a search for proof, not a build of everything. We test the problem with real buyers, ship the smallest product someone will pay for, and design the foundations — tenancy, data model, auth and billing — so the next hundred customers do not force a rebuild. For Indian founders selling abroad and international founders building with an Indian team, billing, tax and data-protection choices are made early, because they are expensive to change later.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to build a SaaS MVP?

The cost of a SaaS MVP depends on the number of core workflows, user roles and integrations, whether billing is simple plans or usage-based, how much AI is involved, and the level of security your buyers expect. A single-workflow tool for small businesses and an enterprise product with SSO and audit logs are very different builds. gaa-tha quotes validation and MVP as fixed phases after discovery, and our SaaS product development guide covers what moves the number.

### How long does it take to launch a SaaS product?

A SaaS MVP typically takes ten to sixteen weeks to build after two to four weeks of validation and scoping. The fastest launches cut the first version to one workflow that a specific customer will pay for, then add the rest once real usage data arrives.

### Which tech stack do you use for SaaS products?

Our default SaaS stack is TypeScript end to end — Next.js or React on the front end, Node.js APIs and PostgreSQL — with Python where AI or data work is central, hosted on AWS, Google Cloud, Vercel or Supabase depending on scale and team. We pick well-supported, widely used tools so you can hire for them later, and document every choice.

### What is multi-tenant architecture, and do we need it?

Multi-tenant architecture means one application serves many customer organisations while keeping each one's data separate. Almost every SaaS product needs it; the real question is how strict the separation is — a shared database with tenant-level security for most products, or separate schemas or databases for customers with stricter requirements. Getting this right at the start avoids a painful migration later.

### Which billing setup should a SaaS company in India use?

Indian SaaS companies commonly use Razorpay Subscriptions with UPI Autopay and card mandates for domestic customers, and Razorpay's international payments, Paddle as a merchant of record, or Stripe where their entity is eligible for overseas customers. The right setup depends on where your company is registered, where customers are and how much tax handling you want to outsource. Eligibility and RBI recurring-payment rules change, so we confirm current terms with each provider before wiring billing.

### Will the product be SOC 2, GDPR or DPDP compliant?

Compliance is something your company achieves through an audit or assessment, not something code alone provides, but we build with the practices those frameworks expect: access controls, encryption, audit logs, backups, retention rules, consent records and documented processes. That makes a SOC 2 audit, a GDPR review or India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) obligations far easier when customers start asking. We do not issue certifications; we prepare the product and the paperwork for your auditor.

### Can you add AI features to our SaaS product?

Yes. We add AI where it saves users real time — answering questions over their own data, drafting, classification, summaries and agents that complete multi-step tasks — using Claude, GPT or Gemini models through their APIs. Each feature ships with usage limits, cost tracking, evaluation tests and tenant-level data separation, so AI does not become an uncontrolled bill or a data leak.

### Who owns the code and IP, and what happens after launch?

You own the code, the IP and every cloud and billing account from day one; the repository and infrastructure sit in your organisation. After launch most clients keep a monthly product-team retainer for releases, monitoring and scaling, while some move development in-house — the system is documented so that handover is straightforward.
