## Key takeaways

- Judge partners on live work you can verify and on the people who will actually do your work, not on the pitch deck.
- Agree time-zone overlap, communication tools and response times in writing before the project starts.
- Protect yourself with an NDA, IP transfer on payment, non-solicitation and accounts held in your own name.
- A small paid pilot reveals more about quality and communication than any number of sales calls.
- Use one weighted scorecard for every candidate, and run the first 90 days as a structured trial.

## What should you check before hiring an outsourcing partner in India?

Check five things before anything else: live work you can verify, references you can call, who will actually do the work, how the partner protects your data and IP, and whether their working hours overlap with yours.

India has a deep pool of agencies, studios and freelancers across marketing, design, Shopify, web, mobile and ERP development. That depth is the advantage and the challenge: the gap between a strong partner and a weak one is wide, and both can look similar on a website. The checklist below is designed for US and UK founders, CTOs, operations leads and agency owners who need to separate them quickly, whether you are outsourcing for your own company or white-labeling work for your clients.

Start by deciding which type of partner you need, because each is right for a different job:

| Partner type | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Freelancer | Small, well-defined tasks and quick fixes | Availability, no backup, limited range of skills |
| Specialist studio | Deep work in one area, such as Shopify builds, video editing or paid media | You may need other vendors for everything around it |
| Full-service agency | Work that spans strategy, creative, development and marketing under one plan | Confirm each skill is in-house rather than subcontracted |
| Development company | Larger software builds, web apps, mobile apps and ERP implementations | Product and marketing context; ownership of business outcomes |
| White-label partner | Agencies that resell work under their own brand | Confidentiality, unbranded delivery and non-solicitation terms |

## What does a complete outsourcing partner checklist include?

A complete checklist covers capability, people, process, protection and commercial terms. Work through it for every shortlisted partner.

- **Portfolio and live work.** Links to live websites, stores, apps or ad accounts they worked on, with a clear statement of what they did on each. Screenshots alone are not enough.

- **References.** At least two past or current clients you can speak to, ideally in your country or with a similar time difference.

- **Who does the work.** Whether the team is in-house or work is subcontracted to freelancers, and the names and roles of the people assigned to you.

- **Time-zone coverage.** The exact hours they are online in your time zone. UK hours overlap naturally with the Indian afternoon; US coverage requires a team that deliberately works shifted hours.

- **Communication tools.** Slack or Teams for conversation, a project board such as Jira, Linear, ClickUp or Asana for tasks, and video calls for reviews.

- **Security and NDA.** Willingness to sign your NDA before receiving details, named accounts with two-factor authentication and least-privilege access.

- **IP and account ownership.** Code in your repository, ad accounts, stores, domains and app store accounts in your name, and IP transfer on payment written into the contract.

- **Contract terms.** Scope, change requests, warranty, confidentiality, non-solicitation of your staff or clients, termination and handover.

- **Payment methods.** Invoices you can pay easily from abroad, such as international bank wire (SWIFT), PayPal or card through a processor like Stripe, with clear currency terms.

- **Pilot project.** Openness to a small paid trial before a larger build or retainer.

- **Reporting.** Sample weekly updates and monthly reports, so you know what you will see and when.

Ask for evidence rather than assurances for each item: a sample report, a redacted contract, a screenshot of how they structure a project board, or a short call with the person who would lead your account. Partners who are organized can produce these quickly.

## How do you score outsourcing partners side by side?

Score every candidate on the same weighted criteria, from 1 to 5, before you look at price; then compare the price of the top two or three.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant live work | 20% | Several live examples close to your project, with specifics on what they did |
| Team and seniority | 15% | Named in-house people with relevant experience; senior oversight on your account |
| Communication and overlap | 15% | Clear written English, fast replies in the trial, real overlap with your working hours |
| Process and quality | 15% | Discovery, written specs, staging, QA, documentation and a warranty period |
| Security, IP and ownership | 10% | NDA up front, accounts in your name, IP transfer and access controls in the contract |
| References | 10% | Clients who confirm delivery, honesty about problems and how they were fixed |
| Commercial fit | 10% | A transparent pricing model, clear assumptions and payment methods that suit you |
| Pilot result | 5% | Pilot delivered on time, to brief, with useful questions along the way |

Adjust the weights to your situation. An agency white-labeling work for its clients may weight communication, confidentiality and non-solicitation higher; a startup building an MVP may weight product thinking and seniority higher.

## What questions should you ask on the first call?

Ask questions that force specific answers about people, process and past work; vague answers to these are the most reliable early warning.

1. Which three projects are most similar to ours, and what exactly did your team do on each?

2. Who will work on our account, are they employees, and what happens if one of them leaves?

3. Which hours will your team be online in our time zone, and who answers urgent issues?

4. How do you turn our brief into a written scope, and who signs it off?

5. What does a typical week look like: updates, demos, reports?

6. Where will the code, designs and accounts live, and how do you hand them over at the end?

7. Will you sign our NDA and agree to non-solicitation and IP transfer in the contract?

8. How do you handle change requests, and how are they priced?

9. What went wrong on a recent project, and what did you change afterward?

10. Can we start with a small paid pilot?

For agencies outsourcing client work, add: how do you keep work unbranded, and do you ever contact the end client? For contract terms specific to white-label arrangements, see [white-label agreements explained](https://gaa-tha.com/blog/white-label-agreements-nda-non-solicitation).

## What are the red flags when choosing an Indian outsourcing partner?

The strongest red flag is a partner that cannot point to live work and explain what they personally delivered. Others to take seriously:

- Accounts, repositories or ad accounts set up in the partner's name instead of yours.

- Reluctance to sign an NDA, agree IP transfer or put non-solicitation in writing.

- A different, more junior team appears after you sign, or the people on the sales call are never seen again.

- Undisclosed subcontracting to freelancers.

- Quotes produced in minutes without questions about your goals or constraints.

- Guaranteed rankings, ROAS, sales numbers or app store features.

- No staging environment, test plan, reporting sample or warranty period.

- Replies that take days during the sales process; it rarely gets faster after signing.

- Unusual payment demands, such as large upfront payments for undefined scope or requests to pay personal accounts.

## How should you run a paid pilot project?

Run a pilot as a small, real piece of work with a fixed scope, a deadline and clear acceptance criteria, paid at the partner's normal terms so you see their normal behavior.

- **Development:** a landing page, a custom Shopify section, a bug-fix batch or one feature behind a staging link.

- **Performance marketing:** an ad account audit with a prioritized action plan, or one campaign build for review before launch.

- **Social and video:** a batch of posts or a set of edited reels from your raw footage and brand guidelines.

- **ERP:** a process-mapping workshop and written blueprint for one department.

Judge the pilot on more than the output: the questions they asked, how they handled feedback, whether updates arrived without chasing and whether the deadline held.

Set the pilot up the way the real engagement will run: the same tools, the same contacts, the same reporting rhythm and the same contract terms. A pilot run informally over chat tells you little about how a six-month retainer will feel. Write down before it starts what would make you continue, what would make you stop, and what you would want changed, then share that with the partner so the evaluation is fair on both sides.

Running pilots with two finalists in parallel is worth the extra cost when the engagement is large. The comparison is usually decisive, and the losing pilot still produces useful work.

## How should you run the first 90 days with a new partner?

Treat the first 90 days as a structured trial with written goals for each month, so both sides know what good looks like and problems surface early.

| Period | Focus | What you should see |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1 to 30 | Onboarding and access | Accounts and tools set up in your name, a written scope or plan, communication rhythm agreed, first deliverables on a staging link or for review |
| Days 31 to 60 | Delivery rhythm | Work shipping on schedule, weekly updates without chasing, quality issues raised and fixed, change requests handled through the agreed process |
| Days 61 to 90 | Review and decide | A review against the goals you set, feedback in both directions, a decision to expand, adjust or end the engagement |

Keep a short shared log of decisions and issues. If the relationship ends, it makes the handover easier; if it continues, it becomes the basis for a longer retainer.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I find a reliable outsourcing partner in India?

Shortlist partners with live work similar to your project, call their references, confirm who will actually do the work, and check their working-hours overlap with your team. Then run a small paid pilot with two finalists and score both on the same criteria before deciding.

### Is it better to hire a freelancer or an agency in India?

A freelancer suits a small, well-defined task with a tight budget. An agency suits ongoing work, projects that need several skills such as design, development and marketing, and situations where you need backup if one person is unavailable.

### How do I protect my intellectual property when outsourcing?

Sign an NDA before sharing details, keep code and accounts in your own name from the first day, and make the contract transfer IP in the deliverables to you on payment. Give named, least-privilege access and remove it when people leave the project.

### What contract terms should an outsourcing agreement include?

Scope and deliverables, change-request process, timelines, payment terms, confidentiality, IP transfer, non-solicitation, warranty or bug-fix period, termination and handover obligations. Agencies white-labeling work should add unbranded delivery and no contact with the end client. Have your own counsel review it.

### How do I pay an outsourcing partner in India from the US or UK?

Most Indian agencies accept international bank wires (SWIFT), and many also accept PayPal or card payments through a processor such as Stripe. Agree the invoice currency, payment schedule and who bears transfer fees before work starts.

### Should I start with a pilot project?

Yes. A small paid pilot with a fixed scope and deadline shows the partner's quality, communication and reliability in practice, at low risk. Use the result as one of your scorecard criteria rather than relying on sales calls alone.
