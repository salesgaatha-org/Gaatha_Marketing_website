## What is Conversion Rate Optimisation (CRO)?

Conversion rate optimisation (CRO) is the practice of increasing the share of website visitors who complete a purchase, by finding where they hesitate or leave and fixing it with evidence. It uses analytics, heatmaps, session recordings and customer feedback to diagnose problems, then tests changes to product pages, cart, checkout, offers and trust signals to see which actually lift orders.

For Indian D2C stores the usual leaks are predictable: slow mobile pages, product pages that answer none of the buyer's questions, no clarity on size or ingredients, missing COD and delivery information, a cart with no reason to add more and a checkout with too many steps. gaa-tha fixes the obvious problems first, then runs A/B tests on the changes that carry risk, so every improvement is measured rather than assumed.

## How we work

1. **Measure** — We verify tracking, map the funnel from landing to purchase and find the steps and devices where visitors leave.
2. **Diagnose** — Heatmaps, recordings, customer-support chats and reviews explain why people leave at those steps.
3. **Fix the obvious** — Broken, slow or confusing elements are fixed straight away without a test, because the evidence is already clear.
4. **Test the rest** — Riskier changes — offers, layouts, pricing presentation — run as A/B tests with a hypothesis and a decision rule.
5. **Report and repeat** — Monthly results by device and source, winning changes made permanent, and the next set of hypotheses agreed.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good ecommerce conversion rate in India?

It depends on category, price point and traffic source, so any single benchmark is misleading; stores selling low-ticket consumables convert far better than furniture or jewellery, and returning visitors convert several times better than cold ad traffic. The useful comparison is your own rate by device and source, month on month. gaa-tha sets a baseline in the audit and reports against it.

### How long before CRO shows results?

The audit takes about two weeks and the first fixes usually go live in the same month. Tests need enough orders to reach a decision, which for most Indian D2C stores means two to six weeks per test, so a meaningful programme runs for at least three months.

### Do I need a lot of traffic for A/B testing?

For tests, yes — a few hundred orders a month is a practical minimum to reach decisions in reasonable time. Below that, we rely on analytics, recordings and best-practice fixes rather than split tests, and measure before-and-after conversion instead.

### What are the most common conversion problems on Indian D2C stores?

Slow mobile pages, product pages that leave buyer questions unanswered, unclear delivery time and COD availability, missing reviews, no reason to add a second item to the cart, and long checkouts. Most stores have three or four of these, and fixing them comes before any testing.

### Should I fix conversion before spending more on ads?

Usually, yes. Every improvement in conversion rate lowers cost per purchase across all channels at once, so a store that converts poorly wastes ad budget. We often run CRO and performance marketing together so that landing pages and ads are improved as one system.

### Do you change the design of my store?

Only where the evidence says to. CRO works within your brand and theme — reordering information, adding trust elements, simplifying steps — and is not a redesign. When a full redesign is the right answer, we say so and scope it separately.

### Which tools do you use for CRO?

GA4 for the funnel, Microsoft Clarity or Hotjar for heatmaps and recordings, Shopify or WooCommerce features and testing apps for experiments, and review platforms such as Judge.me for social proof. All are set up in your own accounts.
