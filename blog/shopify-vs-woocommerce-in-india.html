Shopify vs WooCommerce in India: which should you build on?

By gaa-tha· 25 September 2026· 4 min read· Commerce
Blog cover · 16:9

For most Indian D2C brands, Shopify is the better choice: faster to launch, easier to maintain, and well supported by Indian checkout, payment and logistics integrations. WooCommerce suits brands that want full control, already run WordPress, or want to avoid subscription costs and can manage hosting and updates. The decision rests on who will maintain the store and how much customisation the business genuinely needs.

Key takeaways

  • Shopify: hosted, fast, reliable, subscription plus app costs.
  • WooCommerce: flexible and free at the core, but you own hosting, security and updates.
  • Indian checkout and payment integrations are strong on both, with Shopify’s ecosystem more turnkey.
  • Choose by maintenance capacity and customisation needs, not by platform loyalty.

How do Shopify and WooCommerce differ in practice?

AspectShopifyWooCommerce
Hosting and securityIncluded and managedYour responsibility or your host’s
Speed to launchDays to weeksWeeks, depending on theme and plugins
CustomisationThemes, Liquid, custom sections and appsUnlimited through code and plugins
Cost structureMonthly subscription plus apps and payment feesFree core; hosting, plugins, development and maintenance
MaintenanceLowOngoing updates and compatibility checks

Plans and fees change; check current pricing for both before deciding.

Which handles Indian payments, checkout and logistics better?

Both support UPI, cards, wallets and COD through Indian payment gateways, and both integrate with shipping aggregators. Shopify’s ecosystem of one-click checkout providers, COD verification tools and pincode logic is more plug-and-play; WooCommerce can do all of it with plugins and some development. For brands that want a smooth checkout on day one with minimal engineering, Shopify is the path of least resistance. Our Shopify development work covers checkout integrations and COD rules; ecommerce website development covers WooCommerce and custom builds.

When does WooCommerce or a custom build make more sense?

When the business already runs a content-heavy WordPress site and wants commerce inside it; when the catalogue or pricing logic is unusual (complex B2B pricing, configurators, quotation flows); when the team has developers who will maintain the stack; or when subscription costs matter more than time. A manufacturer selling to trade buyers with tiered pricing may fit here better than on Shopify.

What should decide it for your brand?

Ask who will maintain the store in month six, how many custom features are truly required, and how quickly you need to launch. A consumer brand with a standard catalogue and a small team should choose Shopify; a business with technical capacity and unusual requirements should consider WooCommerce or custom. Either way, speed, tracking and conversion work matter more than the platform label. The Kitcoz store, with its combo builder, pincode checker and checkout integration, was built on Shopify because those features were achievable quickly within the platform. Ongoing store management keeps either platform healthy.
