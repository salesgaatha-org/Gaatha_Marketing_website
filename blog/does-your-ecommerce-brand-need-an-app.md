# Does your ecommerce brand need an app? PWA vs builders vs native

> Most ecommerce brands do not need a mobile app until repeat customers drive a large share of revenue. An app pays when people buy often, respond to push notifications and value loyalty perks; it rarely helps win new customers. Before that point, a fast mobile site or a PWA does the job. When an app is justified, choose between a Shopify app builder for speed and a custom Flutter or React Native app on the Shopify Storefront API for control.

## Key takeaways

- An app is a retention tool: it earns its cost from customers who already buy, not from new ones.
- Rule of thumb: consider an app when repeat buyers bring in roughly a third or more of revenue and order several times a year.
- A PWA or a faster mobile site is the right first step for most brands; Shopify app builders are the fastest route to a real app.
- Custom Flutter or React Native apps on the Shopify Storefront API make sense for unique experiences, B2B ordering and scale.
- Judge the app on incremental orders against a comparable group of web customers, not on installs or raw app revenue.

## When does an ecommerce app pay for itself?

An ecommerce app pays for itself when it makes existing customers order more often, because almost everyone who installs a brand’s app is already a customer. The question is not “will people download it?” but “will the app create orders that would not have happened on the website?”

The signals that an app is likely to pay:

- **High order frequency.** Groceries, staples, coffee, pet food, personal care refills, baby products and fast-moving fashion drops, where customers buy monthly or more.

- **A meaningful repeat base.** As a rule of thumb, repeat buyers contribute roughly a third or more of revenue and number in the thousands, not hundreds.

- **A loyalty or membership model.** Points, tiers, early access or subscriptions give people a reason to keep the app installed.

- **Push-worthy moments.** Restocks, limited drops, replenishment reminders and order updates that customers actually want to receive.

- **Logged-in behaviour.** Customers who reorder from history, save carts or manage subscriptions benefit from a persistent, signed-in experience.

The economics are simple: incremental orders from push, reorder and loyalty, multiplied by contribution margin, must exceed the yearly cost of the app plus the team time to run it. Retention flows on WhatsApp and email, covered in our [D2C ecommerce growth guide](https://gaa-tha.com/blog/d2c-ecommerce-growth-guide), do much of the same job for less, so an app should add something those channels cannot.

## When is an app a waste of money for an ecommerce brand?

An app is a waste of money when customers buy rarely, when the mobile website is not yet converting, or when nobody will run the app after launch. The common failure patterns:

- **Low purchase frequency.** Furniture, mattresses, appliances and one-off gifts rarely justify an app; customers will not keep it installed for a purchase every two years.

- **Expecting the app to bring new customers.** Paid app installs are usually more expensive than website traffic, and a new visitor will not install an app before trusting the brand.

- **A leaky mobile site.** If mobile web conversion, speed and checkout are weak, fix those first; our [Shopify speed and conversion checklist](https://gaa-tha.com/blog/shopify-speed-and-conversion-checklist) is a good starting point.

- **No owner for push and CRM.** An app without a weekly push and offer calendar becomes a dormant icon, then an uninstall.

- **Tier-2 storage reality.** Many Indian buyers use budget Android phones with limited storage and delete apps they do not open each week.

The better order of operations is: make the mobile site fast and the checkout smooth, set up WhatsApp and email retention flows, launch a loyalty or subscription offer on the website, and only then ask whether an app would add something new. ## What are the options: PWA, app builder or custom app?

There are four ways to give customers an app-like experience, and they differ mainly in control, cost and time to launch.

| Option | What it is | Time to launch | Cost profile | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Progressive web app (PWA) | Your website made installable, with offline caching and web push | Days to weeks | Lowest; part of web development | On iPhone, web push works only after the customer adds the site to the home screen (iOS 16.4 and later); weaker app store presence |
| Shopify app builder | Services such as Tapcart, Vajro, Plobal Apps or Appbrew that turn a Shopify store into iOS and Android apps | 2–6 weeks | Monthly subscription that rises with plan and features; check current pricing | Template-bound design, limited custom logic, ongoing fee, dependency on the vendor |
| Custom cross-platform app | A Flutter or React Native app on the Shopify Storefront API and Customer Account API | 3–5 months | Indicatively ₹8–25 lakh to build in India, plus maintenance | Higher upfront cost; needs a team to maintain it |
| Fully native app | Separate Swift and Kotlin apps | 5–8 months | Highest | Rarely justified for ecommerce alone |

Three practical notes for Indian Shopify stores. First, checkout: Shopify’s Checkout Kit presents Shopify checkout inside a native app, but stores that use a third-party checkout such as GoKwik, Shiprocket Checkout or Razorpay Magic Checkout need to confirm that provider’s mobile app support before choosing a route. Second, store fees: Apple and Google generally do not take a commission on physical products, so your existing gateway and UPI, card and COD options continue to apply. Third, data: a custom app reads products, prices and inventory from the same Shopify store, so nothing is managed twice. ## Do distributors and B2B brands need ordering and field-sales apps?

B2B ordering and field-sales apps often justify themselves faster than consumer apps, because retailers and sales reps order every week and the app replaces phone calls, WhatsApp photos of order slips and manual entry.

A **retailer ordering app** lets shops and stockists see their own price list, schemes and credit limit, reorder from history, track dispatch and download GST invoices. A **field-sales app** gives reps their beat plan, outlet list, order capture with offline mode for low-network areas, photo proof of display, payment collection and attendance with location. Both need to sync with the system of record, usually Tally, Zoho, SAP Business One, Odoo, ERPNext or a custom ERP, and most send order confirmations on WhatsApp.

Shopify’s native B2B features (fully available on higher plans) cover company accounts, price lists and payment terms for brands already on Shopify. ## Which option should you choose?

Choose the lightest option that serves your most frequent customers well; move up only when the numbers justify it.

| Your situation | Recommended option |
|---|---|
| New or growing D2C brand, mostly first-time buyers | Fast mobile site, WhatsApp and email retention; no app yet |
| Strong repeat base, standard catalogue, small team | PWA, then a Shopify app builder |
| High-frequency category with loyalty or subscriptions | App builder to test, custom app once app revenue is proven |
| Unique experience: configurators, kit builders, try-on, content and community | Custom Flutter or React Native app on the Storefront API |
| Multi-brand or multi-city store with local delivery slots | Custom app with store and slot logic |
| Retailers or distributors reordering weekly | Custom B2B ordering app linked to Tally or ERP, or Shopify B2B |
| Field sales team taking orders at outlets | Custom field-sales app with offline mode |

## Which metrics show whether your app is working?

An app is working if it creates incremental orders at a profit, and the only reliable way to see that is to compare app users with a similar group of web customers. App users are your most loyal customers to begin with, so raw “app users spend more” figures overstate the effect.

1. **Incremental repeat rate:** repeat purchase rate and order frequency of app users versus customers with a similar order history who did not install, measured over 90 days.

2. **Share of revenue from the app** and whether total revenue from those customers grew or simply moved channel.

3. **App conversion rate** versus mobile web conversion.

4. **Push opt-in rate** and revenue per push sent; falling opt-ins mean messages are not wanted.

5. **30- and 90-day retention** of active app users, and uninstall rate after campaigns.

6. **Crash-free sessions** above 99% and app store rating.

7. **Payback:** months until incremental contribution margin covers the build and running costs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a PWA good enough instead of a mobile app?

For most ecommerce brands, yes, at least at first. A PWA is installable, fast and supports web push, though on iPhone push works only after the customer adds it to the home screen. Move to a native or cross-platform app when repeat customers and loyalty features justify it.

### How much does a Shopify mobile app cost?

Shopify app builders charge a monthly subscription that rises with plan and features, so check current pricing. A custom Flutter or React Native app on the Shopify Storefront API typically costs ₹8–25 lakh to build in India as an indicative 2026 range, plus 15–25% a year to maintain.

### Will a mobile app increase my ecommerce sales?

Only if it makes existing customers buy more often. Apps mainly lift repeat purchases through push notifications, faster reordering and loyalty; they rarely bring new customers on their own.

### Do Apple and Google take a commission on products sold in my app?

Generally no for physical products and real-world services, which use your own payment gateway. Store billing and commissions usually apply to digital goods and subscriptions, and rules vary by region, so check current policy.

### Can my app use GoKwik, Razorpay or Shiprocket checkout?

Often yes, but confirm with the provider first. Shopify’s Checkout Kit brings Shopify checkout into native apps, while third-party checkouts each have their own mobile support, which decides how your app checkout is built.

### How do I get customers to download my brand’s app?

Ask existing customers at the moments they care about: order confirmation, delivery updates and loyalty rewards. Offer something app-only that is genuinely useful, such as early access or faster reordering, rather than buying installs from strangers.
