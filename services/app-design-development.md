## What is Web & Mobile App Design?

Web and mobile app design is the work of deciding what an app does for its users and how it feels to use — the flows, screens, states, interactions and visual system — and documenting it so developers can build it exactly. UI is what the user sees; UX is whether they get where they need to go without thinking. Good app design is decided in flows and prototypes, long before code.

At gaa-tha app design is treated as a product problem with a brand on top. We map the jobs the app must do, wireframe the flows, test a clickable prototype with real users, then design the full UI in your identity as a component-based system with tokens developers can implement. Admin consoles and dashboards get the same care, because the people running the business use them every day.

## How we work

1. **Discovery** — We interview users and stakeholders, list the jobs the app must do and the business rules behind them, and agree scope and success metrics.
2. **Flows and wireframes** — User flows and low-fidelity wireframes are drawn for every journey, reviewed with your team and corrected before any visual design.
3. **Prototype and test** — A clickable prototype is tested with a handful of real users; confusion and dead ends are fixed while they are still cheap to fix.
4. **UI and design system** — Every screen and state is designed in your brand as a component-based system with tokens, ready for development.
5. **Build and handover** — Our engineers build and launch the web or mobile app from the design system, or we hand specifications and assets to your developers and support them through build and QA.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is UI/UX design?

UX (user experience) design decides how an app works — the flows, structure and logic that get a user to their goal without friction. UI (user interface) design decides how it looks — layout, colour, type, components and states. gaa-tha does both, testing the UX in a clickable prototype before the UI is finalised in your brand.

### What is the difference between a website and a web app?

A website presents information and captures enquiries; a web app lets logged-in users do things — place orders, track jobs, manage inventory, view dashboards. Web apps need user roles, data, integrations and many more screen states, which is why they are designed as systems rather than pages.

### How long does app design take?

Three to six weeks for UX and UI on a typical app or admin console: about a week each for discovery and flows, one to two for the prototype and testing, and one to three for full UI and the design system. Web app development then takes six to twelve weeks depending on scope and integrations.

### Do you develop the app as well as design it?

Yes. Web apps, admin consoles, dashboards and iOS and Android apps (Flutter, React Native or native) are built in-house by the same team — see Mobile App Development and SaaS Product Development. If you have your own developers, we deliver a complete developer-ready package and stay on through their build and QA.

### How much does app design cost in India?

It depends on the number of screens, user roles, integrations and whether you need design only or design plus development, so we quote by phase after a discovery call rather than a flat rate. Design-only engagements can start small with a prototype and grow as the product is validated.

### What do we receive at the end?

Editable Figma files with flows, prototype, all screens and a design system; a handover package with specifications, assets and interaction notes; and, if we build it, a launched web app with documentation and all credentials in your name.

### Can you redesign an existing app or admin panel?

Yes. We audit the current product with real users and analytics, keep what works, fix the flows that cause drop-off or support tickets, and rebuild the UI as a consistent system. Redesigns are phased so development can ship improvements without a big-bang relaunch.

### Do you design internal tools like admin consoles and dashboards?

Yes, and they are often the most valuable work: order-management consoles, distributor portals, sales dashboards and content admins that your team uses every day. For smaller, single-purpose tools see Custom Tools & Automations.
