# Website development cost in India: a 2026 pricing guide

> In 2026, most Indian businesses should budget roughly ₹50,000–₹2 lakh for a professionally built 5–10 page website, ₹1.5–10 lakh for a corporate or B2B site, ₹75,000–2.5 lakh for a first Shopify store and ₹5 lakh upward for a custom web app. These are indicative market ranges, not quotes. Then set aside 15–25% of the build cost every year for hosting, maintenance and content, because that is what keeps the site earning.

## Key takeaways

- The same “10-page website” can honestly cost ₹25,000 or ₹4 lakh; the difference is strategy, original design, copywriting, integrations and who is accountable after launch.
- Budget for the whole first year: build, plus domain, hosting, email, licences, content and maintenance, plus 18% GST on Indian invoices.
- Content (copy, photography, product data) is the most commonly forgotten cost and the most common cause of delay.
- Brief every bidder with the same one-page document and compare quotes line by line, not by the total.
- Save money by phasing features, not by cutting speed, tracking, content quality or ownership of your accounts.

## How much does a website cost in India in 2026?

A website in India costs anywhere from about ₹10,000 for a freelancer-built landing page to ₹60 lakh or more for a custom web application built by a full-service agency or engineering firm. The table shows typical 2026 market ranges by website type and by who builds it.

| Website type | Usually includes | Freelancer | Small studio | Full-service agency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Landing page | One page, enquiry form or WhatsApp button, tracking | ₹10,000–40,000 | ₹30,000–1 lakh | ₹75,000–2 lakh |
| Small business website | 5–10 pages, theme or light custom design, editable CMS, forms | ₹20,000–75,000 | ₹50,000–2 lakh | ₹1.5–4 lakh |
| Corporate or B2B website | 15–50 pages, product catalogue, RFQ forms, CRM link, two or more languages | ₹75,000–2 lakh | ₹1.5–5 lakh | ₹3–12 lakh+ |
| Portfolio or brand website | Original design, case studies, motion, CMS | ₹25,000–1.5 lakh | ₹1–4 lakh | ₹2.5–10 lakh |
| Ecommerce store (Shopify or WooCommerce) | Theme setup to custom theme, gateways, COD rules, shipping, apps | ₹30,000–1.5 lakh | ₹1–5 lakh | ₹3–15 lakh |
| Custom web app or portal (MVP) | Logins, dashboards, workflows, integrations | ₹2–8 lakh | ₹5–25 lakh | ₹15–60 lakh+ |

**These are indicative 2026 Indian market ranges, not a quote and not a gaa-tha price list.** They exclude 18% GST, domain, hosting and paid third-party software. Treat the ranges as a sense check: a quote far below the bottom of a range usually leaves something out, and one far above should explain what extra it delivers.

## Why do freelancers, studios and agencies quote so differently?

Quotes differ because you are buying different amounts of strategy, design, writing, engineering and accountability, not just “a website”. A website is effort in disguise: a 10-page site with original design and written content is typically 60–150 hours of discovery, structure, copy, design, development, testing and launch work.

- **Freelancer.** One person, often strongest at one skill, usually development or design. Lowest cost and direct contact; you supply content, project management and decisions, and you depend on one person’s availability.

- **Small studio.** Two to ten people covering design and development, sometimes copy. A good middle ground for brand and small business sites; check who handles content and what happens after launch.

- **Full-service digital agency.** Strategy, branding, copywriting, photography, development, SEO and marketing under one roof. Higher cost, but one accountable team and a site connected to the campaigns that will send it traffic.

- **Product engineering firm.** Larger engineering teams for web apps, portals and SaaS. Strong process and scale; often more than a marketing site needs.

Indicative hourly rates help explain the spread: experienced Indian freelancers often charge somewhere around ₹500–2,500 an hour and agencies a blended ₹1,500–4,000, depending on seniority and city. International buyers, including our clients in Canada, usually find Indian quotes well below North American rates; compare scope, seniority and time-zone overlap rather than the hourly number. ## What drives the cost of a website?

Ten factors decide most of a website’s price, and the first three usually matter most.

1. **Number of unique templates, not pages.** Fifty product pages on one template cost far less than ten pages that each need a different layout.

2. **Design originality.** A configured theme, a customised theme and a fully original design in Figma are three different price points.

3. **Content.** Whether the provider writes the copy, plans the structure and produces photography, or you supply everything.

4. **Integrations.** CRM (Zoho, HubSpot), ERP or Tally, payment gateways such as Razorpay, the WhatsApp Business API, booking systems, maps and search each add scope and testing.

5. **Platform.** Custom code costs more to build and less to run; WordPress, Shopify and Webflow the reverse. See [custom vs WordPress vs Shopify vs Webflow](https://gaa-tha.com/blog/custom-website-vs-wordpress-vs-shopify-vs-webflow).

6. **Languages.** Hindi, Gujarati, Arabic or French versions need translation, hreflang and layout testing.

7. **Motion and interactivity.** Scroll animation, video, 3D or configurators add design and performance work.

8. **Ecommerce logic.** Bundles, COD rules by pincode, B2B pricing, subscriptions and custom checkout flows.

9. **Quality bar.** Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA), Core Web Vitals targets, structured data and cross-device testing take time that cheap quotes skip.

10. **Timeline.** A hard launch date that compresses a six-week project into three adds people and cost.

## What does it cost to run a website each year?

Running a typical small or corporate website in India costs a few thousand rupees a year at the minimum and ₹1–6 lakh a year with active maintenance and content. A useful planning rule is 15–25% of the build cost each year.

| Recurring item | Indicative 2026 range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Domain (.com, .in, .co.in) | Roughly ₹500–1,500 a year | Register in your company’s name; renewal prices can differ from first-year offers |
| Hosting: static or Jamstack | Free tier to a few thousand rupees a month | Cloudflare, Vercel, Netlify or Firebase; costs rise with traffic and team seats |
| Hosting: shared or managed WordPress | ₹3,000–40,000 a year | Managed hosting adds backups, caching and security |
| SSL certificate | Usually free | Let’s Encrypt or Cloudflare; paid certificates are rarely needed |
| Business email | Per user per month | Zoho Mail, Google Workspace or Microsoft 365; check current pricing |
| Platform plans and licences | Varies | Shopify or Webflow plans, premium plugins, themes, fonts, a headless CMS |
| Maintenance or care plan | ₹3,000–50,000 a month | Updates, backups, monitoring, small edits; scope varies widely |
| Content, SEO and AEO | ₹15,000–1 lakh+ a month | New pages, articles, FAQs and search optimisation |

All figures are indicative and change with providers and exchange rates. Tools billed in US dollars, which includes many SaaS products, move with the rupee, so check the billing currency.

## What does a realistic first-year website budget look like?

A realistic first-year budget adds the build fee, one-off extras and twelve months of running costs, then adds GST. Here is an illustrative example for a mid-sized manufacturer commissioning a 20-page English and Gujarati B2B website from a small studio or agency. The figures are hypothetical planning numbers within the indicative ranges above, not a quote.

| Line item | Illustrative amount |
|---|---|
| Design and development (custom-coded, product catalogue, RFQ form linked to the CRM) | ₹2,50,000 |
| Copywriting and Gujarati translation | ₹50,000 |
| Factory and product photography (one day) | ₹40,000 |
| Domain, hosting and business email for a year | ₹15,000 |
| Care plan, twelve months (₹3,500 a month) | ₹42,000 |
| **Subtotal** | **₹3,97,000** |
| GST at 18% on services billed in India | About ₹71,500 |

Notice that the build is under two-thirds of the first-year cost, and running costs land at about 23% of the build fee, inside the 15–25% planning rule. Owners who budget only for the build fee end up launching without photography, with half-translated pages, or with nobody to fix the contact form when it breaks. Budget the whole year from day one.

## What hidden costs do website quotes leave out?

The costs most often missing from website quotes are content, licences, third-party usage fees and post-launch changes. Ask about each before you sign.

- **Copywriting and translation.** Many quotes assume you supply final text; writing 15 pages yourself takes weeks.

- **Photography and video.** Product, factory, team and project shoots, or licensed stock images.

- **Font and asset licences.** Commercial web font licences are separate from desktop licences.

- **Premium themes, plugins and apps.** Often annual subscriptions, not one-time purchases.

- **Usage-based fees.** WhatsApp Business API message charges, SMS (which in India needs DLT registration), Google Maps API, transactional email and payment gateway fees.

- **Data migration.** Moving products, blog posts, customers and redirects from an old site.

- **Legal and compliance pages.** Privacy policy, terms and cookie consent suited to India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act and, for overseas visitors, GDPR or Canada’s privacy laws.

- **Change requests.** Anything outside the written scope is billed separately; an unclear scope makes this the biggest overrun.

- **GST.** Indian invoices add 18% GST; check whether a quote is inclusive or exclusive.

- **Rebuild risk.** If the code or accounts are not in your name, changing providers can mean paying for the site twice.

## How do you brief a website project to get accurate quotes?

Send every provider the same one-page brief; accurate quotes come from clear inputs, and a shared brief makes quotes comparable.

1. **Business and audience:** what you sell, to whom, in which cities or countries.

2. **The site’s job:** enquiries, sales, recruitment, investor information, showing work. Name the one action that matters most.

3. **Pages and templates:** a draft sitemap, with page types marked (home, service, product, case study, blog, contact).

4. **Content:** what exists, what needs writing, whether photography is needed.

5. **Features and integrations:** forms, WhatsApp, CRM, catalogue, payments, booking, languages.

6. **Examples:** three sites you like and why, and one you dislike.

7. **Platform preference** and who will edit the site after launch.

8. **Budget range and deadline.** Sharing a range saves weeks; good providers will tell you what fits within it.

## How do you compare website quotes fairly?

Compare quotes line by line against your brief, because two totals rarely describe the same work.

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Scope | Number of templates and pages, revisions included, and what counts as a change request |
| Content | Who writes copy, who sources images, who enters content into the CMS |
| Design | Theme, customised theme or original design; mobile designs shown before build |
| Quality | Speed targets, accessibility, cross-device testing, structured data, analytics and form tracking |
| Ownership | Domain, hosting, code and logins in your name; source code handed over |
| Timeline and payments | Milestones tied to approvals, not dates alone; no 100% advance |
| After launch | Warranty period for defects, training, care plan price and scope |
| Taxes and extras | GST, licences, third-party fees and hosting stated separately |

Then look at live sites each provider has built, test them on your own phone and run them through PageSpeed Insights. A cheaper quote that excludes content and tracking is often the more expensive project.

## Where can you save money on a website, and where should you not?

Save by reducing scope and phasing features; never save on the things that make the site work after launch.

**Sensible savings:** launch with the pages that earn enquiries and add the rest later; use a quality theme for a first Shopify store; write first drafts yourself and pay for editing; reuse brand assets and existing photography where they are good enough; defer a second language until the first converts.

**False savings:** skipping copywriting, buying the cheapest hosting, leaving analytics and lead tracking “for later”, letting the developer own the domain or code, ignoring mobile speed, and skipping a maintenance plan on WordPress. Each of these costs more to fix than to do properly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a basic website cost in India?

A basic 5–10 page business website typically costs about ₹20,000–75,000 from a freelancer, ₹50,000–2 lakh from a small studio and ₹1.5–4 lakh from a full-service agency in 2026. These are indicative market ranges, not quotes. The difference is mostly design originality, copywriting, integrations and support after launch.

### Why does a custom website cost more than a template website?

A custom website includes original design, structure and code built for your business, while a template site adapts a design someone else made. Custom work takes more hours of design and development but usually loads faster, fits your content better and avoids paying for features you do not use.

### Is GST charged on website development in India?

Yes, website design and development services billed to Indian clients attract 18% GST. Quotes are often shown before GST, so confirm whether a figure is inclusive. Services exported to overseas clients are generally zero-rated when the provider files a Letter of Undertaking.

### How much does website maintenance cost per month in India?

Website maintenance typically costs ₹3,000–15,000 a month from a freelancer or small studio and ₹10,000–50,000 a month from an agency, depending on scope. A good care plan covers updates, backups, security monitoring, speed checks and a set amount of content changes each month.

### Should I pay the full website cost upfront?

No. Pay in milestones tied to approvals, for example an advance on signing, a payment on design approval, a payment on staging or testing, and the balance on launch. Keep a final payment for after the domain, code and logins have been handed over to you.

### How long does it take to build a website?

A landing page takes about a week, a corporate or brand website two to five weeks, and a larger site with catalogues or multiple languages six to eight weeks. Content and approvals, not development, usually decide the timeline.

### Is it cheaper to hire an Indian web development company from Canada or abroad?

Usually yes on rates, but compare the full scope, seniority of the team and working-hour overlap rather than the hourly figure. Insist on the same contract essentials as at home: written scope, milestone payments, and code and accounts in your name.
