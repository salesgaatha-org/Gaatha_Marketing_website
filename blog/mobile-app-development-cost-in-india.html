Mobile app development cost in India (2026): Flutter vs native

By gaa-tha· 22 October 2026· 12 min read· Websites & Apps
Blog cover · 16:9

In 2026 a mobile app built in India typically costs ₹3–8 lakh for a simple app, ₹8–25 lakh for a mid-complexity app with accounts, payments and an admin panel, and ₹25 lakh to ₹1 crore or more for a marketplace or multi-app platform. These are indicative market ranges, not quotes. Features, integrations, backend, design depth and platforms drive the number, and running costs add roughly 15–25% of the build cost every year.

Key takeaways

  • Indicative 2026 Indian market ranges: simple apps ₹3–8 lakh, mid-complexity ₹8–25 lakh, complex and marketplace apps ₹25 lakh and up; scope drives the total far more than hourly rate.
  • The backend, admin panel and integrations often cost as much as the screens customers see, so a quote that ignores them is not a real quote.
  • Flutter or React Native builds iOS and Android from one codebase and suits most business apps; native Swift and Kotlin earn their extra cost for heavy device, performance or platform-specific work.
  • Budget 15–25% of the build cost every year for OS updates, fixes, hosting, store fees and third-party APIs.
  • Ship an MVP around one core journey, measure real usage, then fund phase two from evidence rather than guesses.

How much does it cost to build a mobile app in India in 2026?

A mobile app built in India in 2026 usually costs between ₹3 lakh and ₹1 crore, and where a project lands depends mostly on how many user types, workflows and integrations it has. The table below gives indicative market ranges for a cross-platform build (iOS and Android from one codebase) by an experienced Indian team, including design, backend, admin panel, testing and store launch. They are typical ranges across the market, not a gaa-tha price list or a quote.

ComplexityTypical examplesIndicative cost (INR, 2026)Typical timeline
SimpleCatalogue or content app, booking request app, event app, internal tool with OTP login and a handful of screens₹3–8 lakh6–10 weeks
Mid-complexityEcommerce app, appointment and payments app, loyalty app, field-sales ordering app, customer portal with notifications and an admin panel₹8–25 lakh3–5 months
Complex or marketplaceMulti-sided platform with separate customer, vendor and delivery apps, live tracking, chat, wallets, commissions and analytics₹25 lakh–₹1 crore+6–12 months

For international readers: one lakh is 100,000 rupees and one crore is 100 lakh. Two apps that look similar in a pitch deck can sit in different rows once the roles, rules and integrations are written down, which is why the brief matters more than the headline figure.

What drives the cost of a mobile app?

App cost is driven by six things: features and user roles, integrations, the backend and admin panel, design depth, the number of platforms, and the quality bar. Hourly rates matter less than most buyers expect.

Features and user roles

Every user type (customer, vendor, delivery partner, admin, sales rep) adds its own screens, permissions and edge cases. A marketplace is expensive mostly because it is three apps that must agree with each other.

Integrations

Payment gateways such as Razorpay, PayU or Cashfree, SMS OTP, the WhatsApp Business API, maps, shipping aggregators, Shopify, Tally, Zoho or an ERP each add build and testing time. A poorly documented third-party API can cost more than a whole feature.

Backend and admin panel

Someone has to manage products, users, orders, content and refunds. A backend on Firebase or Supabase is quicker to start; a custom Node.js or Python backend on AWS or Google Cloud suits complex rules and scale. Either way, budget for an admin panel, because spreadsheets do not scale.

Design depth

A clean app built on a component library costs far less than a custom design system with motion, illustrations and accessibility testing. Both are valid; decide based on how much the experience is the product.

Platforms and quality

iOS plus Android plus a web app plus tablet layouts multiplies testing. Offline mode, Hindi or Gujarati language support, automated tests, security reviews and device testing on budget Android phones all raise the quality bar and the cost.

Flutter vs React Native vs native: which should you choose?

For most business apps in 2026, Flutter or React Native is the right choice because one codebase serves both iOS and Android, often for 30–40% less build effort than two separate native apps; native Swift and Kotlin is worth it when the app depends on heavy device features, top-end performance or platform-specific experiences.

FactorFlutterReact NativeNative (Swift / Kotlin)
LanguageDartJavaScript or TypeScriptSwift (SwiftUI) for iOS, Kotlin (Jetpack Compose) for Android
Backed byGoogleMeta, with Expo as the common frameworkApple and Google

PerformanceVery good for most appsVery good, improved by the New ArchitectureBest, especially for graphics, AR and heavy media
Device featuresPlugins; custom native code when neededLibraries; custom native modules when neededImmediate access to every new OS feature
Sharing with webPossible, less commonStrong when the web app is React or Next.jsNone
Hiring in IndiaLarge and growing talent poolLarge pool, overlaps with web React developersTwo specialist skill sets to hire and retain
Relative build costLowerLowerHighest
Best forBrand-led UI, consistent design, ecommerce and booking appsTeams already on React, apps sharing logic with a web productBanking-grade security, AR, camera or Bluetooth-heavy, wearables

Simple decision rules: if your web product is React or Next.js and the same team will own both, choose React Native. If design consistency and a polished custom UI matter most, Flutter is usually the faster route. If the app lives on hardware features, or you are building for Apple Watch or deep platform integrations, go native. Kotlin Multiplatform, which shares business logic while keeping native UI, is a maturing middle path worth considering for teams with Android experience. Our mobile app development team works across all three and will tell you which fits rather than defaulting to one.

What are the recurring costs after the app launches?

An app keeps costing money after launch: expect roughly 15–25% of the original build cost each year for maintenance, plus usage-based hosting, store and API fees.

CostHow it is chargedNotes
Apple Developer ProgramUS$99 a year, billed in local currencyNeeded to publish on the App Store; open it in your company’s name
Google Play ConsoleOne-time US$25 registrationOrganisation accounts need a D-U-N-S number and verification
Store commissionTypically 15–30% on digital goods and subscriptionsPhysical products and real-world services (ecommerce, food, travel) generally sit outside in-app purchase rules; policies are changing by region, so check current terms
Hosting and backendMonthly, scales with users and dataFirebase, Supabase, AWS or Google Cloud; small apps can start at a few thousand rupees a month
Third-party APIsPer useSMS OTP, WhatsApp Business API per-message charges, maps beyond free usage, email, AI model tokens
Payment gatewayPer transactionRazorpay, PayU, Cashfree and others; check current pricing by payment method
Maintenance retainerMonthly or yearlyBug fixes, dependency upgrades, security patches, crash monitoring and small changes

The maintenance line is not optional. Apple and Google release new OS versions every year, Google Play requires apps to target a recent Android API level to keep receiving updates, and payment and login SDKs deprecate old versions. An app that is not maintained for 18 months usually needs a costly catch-up project. A good maintenance plan includes crash monitoring with Firebase Crashlytics or Sentry, monthly dependency updates and a small budget for improvements users ask for.

Why should you build an MVP first?

Building a minimum viable product first is the most reliable way to control app cost, because it puts one complete journey in front of real users before you pay for everything else. An MVP is not a cheap app; it is a narrow one, built properly.

PhaseWhat happensTypical duration
DiscoveryGoals, user journeys, feature list ranked must-have and later, technical plan, clickable prototype2–4 weeks (often 5–10% of the build budget)
MVP buildOne core journey end to end, admin panel, analytics, store launch6–14 weeks
LearnReal usage data, support tickets, interviews, retention by cohort4–8 weeks
Phase twoFeatures users actually asked for, performance and scale workOngoing sprints

A worked example: a regional food brand wants an ordering app with loyalty points, subscriptions, referrals, a recipe section and a delivery tracker. The MVP is browse, order, pay by UPI or card, track status and reorder in two taps, with push notifications. Loyalty and subscriptions follow once reorder rates justify them. How do you brief an app project to get accurate quotes?

An accurate quote needs a brief that describes users, journeys and integrations, not just a list of screens. Send every shortlisted team the same document so the quotes are comparable.

  1. The goal and the number: what the app must achieve and how you will measure it (repeat orders, bookings, hours saved).
  2. Users and roles: every type of user and what each can see and do.
  3. The top five journeys: written step by step, for example “a retailer reorders last week’s stock and pays on credit”.
  4. Must-have versus later: a ranked list, with the MVP line drawn.
  5. Integrations: payment gateway, Shopify, Tally, Zoho, ERP, CRM, WhatsApp, SMS, maps, with links to their API documentation if you have them.
  6. Platforms and languages: iOS, Android, web, tablet; English, Hindi, Gujarati or others.
  7. Admin needs: who manages content, orders, users and reports.
  8. References: two or three apps you like and what specifically you like about them.
  9. Budget band and deadline: a range lets teams propose the right scope instead of guessing.
  10. Ownership: source code repository, cloud accounts, Apple and Google developer accounts and domains in your name.

Red flags in quotes: a fixed price with no discovery, no mention of the backend or admin panel, no testing plan, store accounts opened in the developer’s name, and no maintenance terms. If the app needs a polished interface and prototype first, start with app design and development so the build quote is based on approved screens.

How can you reduce app development cost without cutting quality?

The cheapest way to reduce app cost is to remove scope, not to hire the cheapest team. These moves cut cost without leaving you with an app that has to be rebuilt.

  • Go cross-platform with Flutter or React Native unless a clear native requirement exists.
  • Use managed services for login, storage, notifications and analytics (Firebase, Supabase, OneSignal) instead of building them.
  • Reuse what you already run. An ecommerce app can sit on your Shopify store through the Storefront API, so products, inventory and orders stay in one place; see ecommerce app development and Shopify development.
  • Consider a PWA or web app first when users do not need the app daily; our guide to website development costs in India shows how the numbers compare.
  • Launch in one language and one region, then add Hindi, Gujarati or new cities once the core journey works.
  • Buy, do not build, the hard parts such as chat, video calls and maps where good SDKs exist.
  • Keep internal tools off the app. Many “app” ideas are really internal workflows better served by custom software or an AI agent on WhatsApp.
