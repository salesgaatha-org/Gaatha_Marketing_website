## Key takeaways

- White-label lets an agency sell more services and take on more clients without hiring for every skill.
- The agency owns the client, the brand, the pricing and the final sign-off; the partner stays invisible.
- The most commonly white-labeled work is Shopify and web development, PPC and Meta ads, social media, video editing and SEO.
- Consistent quality comes from process: written briefs, style guides, checklists and an internal review before delivery.
- Protect the model in writing with an NDA, an unbranded-delivery clause and non-solicitation, and test the partner with a paid pilot.

## What does white-label mean for a marketing or development agency?

White-label means a partner does the work and your agency delivers it under your own name, as part of your own service. The end client buys from you, talks to you and is invoiced by you; the partner's name does not appear anywhere in the deliverable.

It is related to, but not the same as, other ways agencies use outside help:

| Arrangement | Who the client sees | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| White-label | Only your agency | Reselling a service you do not staff in-house, or adding capacity for a service you do |
| Disclosed subcontracting | Your agency, with the partner named in the contract or on calls | Specialist work where the client expects to meet the expert |
| Co-branded partnership | Both agencies | Joint pitches and long-term alliances |
| Staff augmentation | Your agency; the partner's people work inside your team | Filling roles for a set period, directed by your own managers |

Agencies usually turn to white-label for two reasons: to offer a service clients keep asking for that the agency does not staff (Shopify development at a social media agency, for example), and to absorb volume without carrying extra payroll through quiet months.

## How does a white-label workflow run day to day?

A white-label workflow runs through the agency's tools and the agency's voice: the agency briefs, the partner produces, and the agency reviews and delivers.

1. **The agency wins and scopes the work.** The client talks only to the agency, which turns the requirement into an internal brief.

2. **NDA and brief.** The partner signs an NDA, then receives a written brief: goals, brand guidelines, access needed, deadlines and acceptance criteria.

3. **Access through the agency.** The partner works in the agency's Slack or Teams, project board and shared drive, using agency-issued logins or staff accounts on the client's platforms. Nothing is sent to the client from a partner-branded address.

4. **Production and the partner's own QA.** The partner builds, checks the work against its checklist and posts it for review on the board.

5. **Agency review.** The agency's account lead reviews against the brief and what they know about the client, and requests changes if needed.

6. **Delivery under the agency's brand.** The agency presents the work in its own templates, decks and reports.

7. **Feedback loop.** Client feedback becomes tasks for the partner, and recurring notes become updates to the style guide or checklist so they do not repeat.

Some agencies let the partner join client calls as members of the agency team, using agency email addresses. That is a choice to make client by client, and it should be written into the agreement rather than improvised.

## How do you keep quality consistent when a partner does the work?

Quality stays consistent when the standard is written down and checked twice: once by the partner before submission and once by the agency before delivery.

- **A style guide per client.** Brand colors, fonts, tone of voice, do's and don'ts, and approved examples of past work.

- **A definition of done.** For a Shopify section: responsive at agreed breakpoints, tested on real devices, no console errors, speed checked. For an ad campaign: naming conventions followed, tracking verified on a test conversion, budgets and audiences double-checked.

- **Checklists, not memory.** Launch, QA and reporting checklists that the partner completes and attaches to each task.

- **Templates.** Report templates, creative templates and handover documents in the agency's branding, so output looks the same whoever made it.

- **A named lead on both sides.** Fewer handoffs means fewer misunderstandings.

- **Metrics on the relationship itself.** First-time-right rate, revision rounds, on-time delivery and response time, reviewed monthly.

Start with a paid pilot on one real piece of work. It tells you more about a partner's quality and communication than any portfolio.

## How do agencies price white-label work and protect their margins?

Agencies usually pay the partner a wholesale price and resell the work at their own price, keeping the difference to cover account management, strategy, review time and risk.

The common pricing models between agency and partner are:

- **Fixed price per project** for builds, migrations and audits.

- **Monthly retainer per client account** for ads management, social media and store management.

- **Per deliverable** for a batch of videos, a set of designs or a landing page.

- **Dedicated team or pod** for a monthly fee covering named people who work only on the agency's accounts.

Margins are more often lost to scope creep than to the partner's price. Protect them by matching the partner's scope exactly to the scope you sold, agreeing how change requests are quoted on both sides, and building your own review time into the price you charge. If you pay a partner by time rather than by deliverable, keep a running budget per client so overruns surface early, not at month end.

Price to your client on value and outcome, not as a simple markup on the partner's invoice. Your client is paying for your strategy, judgment and accountability as much as for the production.

## What are the risks of white-labeling and how do you manage them?

The main risks are slipping quality, a partner contacting your client, delays and unclear ownership, and each one has a practical fix.

| Risk | How to manage it |
|---|---|
| Quality slips and your brand takes the blame | Style guides, checklists, an internal review step and a paid pilot before scaling |
| The partner contacts or poaches your client | NDA, unbranded-delivery and non-solicitation clauses, and communication only through agency channels |
| Delays across time zones | Agreed working hours and response times, and a project board with due dates |
| Security and access problems | Staff accounts with limited permissions, no shared passwords, and access removed at offboarding |
| Over-dependence on one partner | Documentation of everything built, with files and accounts held by the agency or the client |
| Unclear ownership of code and creative | IP assignment on payment, and a record of licenses for fonts, stock images and music |

## How do you choose a white-label partner?

Choose a white-label partner on evidence of the specific work you will resell, the way they communicate, and their willingness to put protections in writing, and only then on price.

- Live examples of the exact service, with an explanation of what they did on each.

- A named lead and a clear team structure, with no undisclosed subcontracting.

- Working hours that match how you serve your clients.

- Willingness to sign an NDA, an unbranded-delivery clause and non-solicitation terms.

- Their own QA checklist, shared with you before you sign.

- Agreement to work inside your tools and your templates.

- A paid pilot offered rather than avoided.

- References from other agencies where possible.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a white-label agency?

A white-label agency produces services that other agencies resell under their own brand. The reselling agency handles the client relationship and pricing, while the white-label partner does the work behind the scenes, unbranded.

### Is white-labeling ethical?

Yes, when it is handled honestly. Agencies routinely use specialists and partners, and the agency remains responsible for quality and outcomes. Check your client contracts for clauses on subcontracting or on where data may be processed, and follow them.

### Will my clients know I use a white-label partner?

Not unless you choose to tell them. A proper white-label setup has the partner working in your tools and templates, with no branding and no direct contact with your client unless you approve it.

### Which services are easiest to white-label?

Development (Shopify, websites and apps), PPC and Meta ads management, social media content, video editing and SEO are the easiest, because they are repeatable and can be reviewed against a clear brief before delivery.

### How should I price white-label services to my clients?

Price on the value and outcome for your client, not only as a markup on the partner's cost. Include your time for strategy, account management and review, and allow a buffer for revisions and change requests.

### How do I test a white-label partner before committing?

Run a paid pilot on one real piece of work with written acceptance criteria. Judge first-time quality, revision rounds, deadlines and communication before moving to a retainer.
